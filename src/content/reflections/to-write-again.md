I have been struggling to write down this piece for quite a while. I think I have been trying to define the perfect structure for my website so finally, when it is done, I can publish my thoughts. I think I was just delaying the process of writing again.

I feel that the last time I wrote something, I was focusing too much into making something useful for the world. I felt as if whatever I published, was going to be the next piece I was going to be proud of. However, a question kept crawling my mind: What is it worth?

Ever since I can recall, I have been in love with writing. Not necessarily essays, but also poems, thoughts, and other forms of expressing our minds. I adore to understand new languages and learn about all the possible ways they are connected. There is just so much, we as species, have develop in order to work together.

With AI taking over everything that means knowledge work, I have lost the spark I felt when I thought of writing. It felt as if every piece of text was just a regurgitated blob of statistically generated strings that somehow made sense. It felt as we started to devalue our own communication, and start optimising towards the machines. Of course, this is not only a tech issue. It is a social issue. It is linked to fascism, capitalism, patriarchy and multiple other fields of knowledge coming from social sciences.

All these thoughts prevented me from writing again. I was afraid that whatever instagram caption I would write would be used for training new AI models that would suggest me what to write on those captions themselves. As if every piece of text was not mine so why bother? But along the way, and along these months, I have realised that if there is a time in which we can make things with passion, it is now.

So this short piece is not a commitment to publish more often but rather to do it with more passion. To do it because it makes me human. Because I feel we are more and more neglecting the political nature of tech. And because when more and more companies want to make models to replace our creativity, I can always go back to my own.

I will write again.
